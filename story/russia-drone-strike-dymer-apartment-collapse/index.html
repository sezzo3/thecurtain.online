Russian Drone Strike Collapses Part of Apartment Block Near Kyiv — At Least 14 Missing

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-22 · 5 min read

A Russian drone struck a five-storey apartment building in the settlement of Dymer, north of Kyiv, at around 5am on Sunday, collapsing a whole entrance section from the first floor to the fifth and starting a fire. Nine people were evacuated from the damaged building, including one child, and firefighters brought the blaze under control. But as rescue teams moved in, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said 14 people were unaccounted for and might still be trapped beneath the rubble — among them, he said, a 12-year-old girl and a seven-year-old boy.

Hours later, the first confirmed death was reported: rescuers recovered the body of a woman from the rubble, according to the Kyiv Regional Military Administration. At least three people were injured — a teenager born in 2010 who suffered multiple shrapnel wounds to an arm and was taken to hospital, a 22-year-old woman who cut her foot and was treated at the scene, and a woman of about 54 treated for shock. The regional military chief, Tymur Tkachenko, later gave a preliminary estimate that 15 people might be trapped and said 11 had now been evacuated; the State Emergency Service had initially put the number possibly trapped at seven or eight. The figures differ because they were given at different stages of a rescue that was still under way, and officials warned the toll could change. The blast wave also damaged a nearby kindergarten and a shop.

Footage released by Ukraine's State Emergency Service showed the scale of the task facing rescuers: crews picking through a mountain of concrete slabs and twisted metal, using an extended ladder and an aerial platform to bring residents down from the damaged upper floors. Dog handlers, pyrotechnicians and heavy engineering equipment were brought in, and Red Cross teams worked alongside the emergency crews. A command centre was set up at the site, with social services and psychologists on hand for families waiting for news of relatives they could no longer reach by phone.

The Dymer strike was part of a wider overnight wave of Russian drone attacks across Ukraine. Ukraine's Air Force said Russia launched 186 attack drones overnight, 161 of which were shot down; 72 of them were jet-powered, according to Ukrainska Pravda. In Kharkiv, a strike drone hit the Kyivskyi district and started a fire; in Chernihiv, a Russian strike set a wood-processing plant ablaze; and in Zaporizhzhia, three people, including two children, were injured in an evening attack.

It also capped a week of heavy bombardment. Zelenskyy said Russian forces had launched around 2,700 drones, close to 1,460 aerial bombs and 165 missiles at 20 Ukrainian regions over the past week, a significant number of them ballistic missiles, including North Korean ones. Just two nights earlier, more than 400 attack drones and over 110 missiles were fired at Ukraine in what Zelenskyy called one of the largest attacks of the war so far — seven people were killed in Kyiv in those strikes, and key bridges across the Dnipro River were damaged. The president renewed his calls for allies to speed up deliveries of interceptor missiles, and urged Washington to authorise transfers of interceptors already paid for through an allied procurement scheme.

Ukraine also struck back overnight, hitting a Russian National Guard facility in the Krasnodar region; five people were reported injured there, including four in Afipsky, a settlement home to an oil refinery that has been targeted before. But in Dymer, the focus on Sunday remained on the rubble: as long as the search continued, the number of missing could still change, and every hour without news made the wait harder for the families gathered at the site. The strike was another reminder that, more than four years into the war, it is residential buildings — and the people asleep inside them — that keep bearing the brunt of the nightly drone campaign.
