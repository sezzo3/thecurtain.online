Russian Missiles Crash in Poland as Tusk Calls It a "Deliberate Provocation"

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-20 · 5 min read

Three Russian-origin objects violated Polish airspace overnight and crashed in southeastern Poland during a massive Russian missile and drone attack on Ukraine, Polish authorities said on Friday. A Kh-101 cruise missile came down near the village of Nowy Lipowiec, a second missile fell near Rudnik nad Sanem, and a jet-powered drone crashed near Mołodycz. No one was injured, and no critical infrastructure was damaged.

Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz told a news conference that Polish air defences had detected eleven objects threatening to enter Polish airspace, three of which crossed the border. The first alerts came at about 1.40am local time. The Kh-101, roughly seven metres long, fell at about 5.40am GMT in a field near Nowy Lipowiec, some 60 kilometres from the Ukrainian border; security camera footage captured the explosion, and a local mayor described black smoke rising "like one large, distinct mushroom cloud." Debris from the drone was found scattered across farmland near Mołodycz, about 50 kilometres south, while the second missile came down near Rudnik nad Sanem, a similar distance to the west.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk left no doubt about who was responsible. "We know for certain that what fell on Polish soil came from Russia," he told reporters. "It does not look like an accident. It appears we are dealing with an intentional provocation." Tusk convened an emergency meeting of ministers, military commanders and the heads of the security services, and said he was in constant contact with NATO leaders about how the alliance would respond. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte is due in Warsaw on Saturday.

Polish F-16 fighters and Swedish Gripen jets were scrambled through the night, supported by an allied refuelling aircraft, while Patriot air-defence systems were activated around Rzeszów-Jasionka airport and near Świdnik, and two NATO AWACS surveillance aircraft operated over Poland. NATO described the scramble as a preventive measure to secure Polish airspace. Kosiniak-Kamysz said Warsaw would formally ask Rutte for additional forces and surveillance capabilities for Operation Eastern Sentry, the NATO mission launched in September 2025 after Russian drones first entered Polish airspace.

The timing, Warsaw said, was no coincidence. The incursion came as thousands of troops from eleven nations take part in Coalition of the Willing exercises in southeastern Poland — a drill running from 9 to 22 November to prepare for a possible postwar mission in Ukraine. "It is no coincidence that these objects fell near the site of military exercises involving the coalition of the willing," Tusk said. The exercises went ahead as planned.

The objects crossed into Poland during the same Russian barrage that struck Kyiv overnight, hitting three bridges and killing at least eight people. Military prosecutors, police and counterintelligence officers are investigating all three crash sites, and the defence minister said the attack on Ukraine that also struck Poland was one of the largest since the war began. It is the gravest test of NATO's eastern flank since the drone incursions of September 2025 — and Warsaw's request for reinforcements will show how quickly the alliance answers.
