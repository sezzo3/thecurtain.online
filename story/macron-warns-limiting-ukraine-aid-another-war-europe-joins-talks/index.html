Macron Warns That Rationing Aid to Ukraine Would Mean 'Another War' as Europe Wins a Seat at the Talks

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-15 · 5 min read

French President Emmanuel Macron warned on Saturday that countries seeking to limit aid to Ukraine want to impose an "unjust peace" and that doing so would be a recipe for another war. The French leader's statement, posted on social media, comes as the United States mediates talks between Ukraine and Russia in a bid to end the war that began with Moscow's full-scale invasion in 2022. "Those who want to ration aid to Ukraine wish underneath it all to force an unjust peace on it," Mr Macron said, "and, by extension, all of Europe. That is the spirit of defeat. That is a recipe for another war. That cannot be France's position."

The warning followed a week in which Europe fought its way into negotiations it had feared would be held over its head. US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff held two days of American-mediated talks between Russian and Ukrainian delegations in Abu Dhabi, the first direct negotiations involving the two sides in nine months, which ended with no breakthrough but with an agreement that European countries would join future rounds. On Thursday, the two envoys met President Vladimir Putin in Moscow and then flew to Dublin, where they briefed Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky at Casement Aerodrome. Mr Kushner said the American side had argued for Europeans to be included in the negotiations, and Mr Zelensky said the United States had made progress with Mr Putin on European participation.

At the European Political Community summit in Dublin on Thursday, attended by almost 50 leaders, Europe chose five countries — France, Germany, Italy, the United Kingdom and Poland — to send representatives to the talks. The move followed a draft Dublin Declaration, initiated by France's foreign minister, setting out that peace in Ukraine cannot be settled without Ukraine and that matters affecting Europe's security cannot be decided without Europeans. "Europe must take full part" in the work to bring about a "fair and lasting peace" in Ukraine, Mr Macron said, adding: "What happens in Ukraine concerns us directly… Helping Ukraine defend itself is protecting France and the French."

The urgency of the argument was underlined by a disclosure from Kyiv. Mr Zelensky said in comments released on Saturday that Washington has suggested Ukraine cede land to Moscow — the Donbas region in the east — in exchange for Ukraine joining NATO. The proposal, if it is on the table, would ask Kyiv to trade territory for the security guarantee it has sought since the invasion, and it drew a sharp response from European leaders who have insisted that no settlement can be imposed on Ukraine. Mr Macron said he believed Mr Putin "does not want to negotiate" and was trying to break Ukraine by stepping up drone and missile strikes against civilians and energy infrastructure as winter closes in.

Europe is backing its words with money and hardware. The European Union has agreed a €90 billion loan to Ukraine for 2026 and 2027, the Council has extended the mandate of its military-assistance mission for Ukraine through 15 November 2028 with a budget of nearly €300 million, and the Commission is preparing its 22nd package of sanctions against Russia. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced further winter support on the ground — 500 generators from EU reserves, four floating bridges from Norway and 200 buses — and the coalition of countries providing military support to Ukraine will meet again in Berlin on 19 November. At the next European Council, on 26–27 November, leaders will discuss Ukraine's urgent financing needs for 2027.

Online, the reaction crossed the usual political lines. A thread on r/worldnews carrying Mr Macron's warning drew more than 5,300 upvotes and 120 comments, and the most-upvoted comment — from a French user who said he had "no affection for Macron" and disapproved of his domestic politics — added that he was "in phase with" the president's geopolitical vision on Russia. What happens next will be decided in the negotiating room Europe has just entered — and on the battlefield. European Council President António Costa said Russia "has yet to demonstrate it prioritises peace over dominance", and that the future of European security "cannot be discussed and decided without Europe". Mr Macron said Ukraine's allies must clearly set out, including to Russia, their postwar vision, and "we must also detail what we expect of the United States for Europe's long-term security". For Kyiv, the test is whether a seat at the table protects it from a settlement drawn up over its head; for Europe, it is whether the unity displayed in Dublin survives the first hard choices of a negotiation.
