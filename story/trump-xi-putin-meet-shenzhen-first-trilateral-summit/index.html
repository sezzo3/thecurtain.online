Three Nuclear Powers, One Room: Trump, Xi and Putin to Meet in Shenzhen in a First

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-14 · 5 min read

Donald Trump will sit down with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen on Thursday, 19 November, the White House confirmed on Friday, in the first three-way meeting of the leaders of the world's three largest nuclear powers. The gathering, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in the southern Chinese technology hub, will bring together three men who have met in pairs many times but have never held formal talks as a trio — and it comes with expectations set deliberately, almost comically, low.

The White House said the meeting would focus on nuclear weapons. Spokeswoman Olivia Wales told reporters that Mr Trump "will discuss the reduction of nuclear weapons in the world", adding that the president believes nuclear proliferation is "very dangerous". A senior administration official who briefed reporters was blunt about what the session would produce: "We do not expect to get to any kind of agreement." The meeting is instead being described as an entry point — a first conversation that could, in time, lead to negotiations on a new arms-control framework between the three countries. The same official said Mr Trump also intends to raise the war in Iran with both leaders, while Russia's war in Ukraine will not be discussed at the trilateral at all.

The timing gives the meeting its weight. The last treaty limiting the nuclear arsenals of the United States and Russia, New START, expired in February, leaving the world's two largest nuclear powers without any binding cap on their arsenals for the first time in decades, and China — whose arsenal is smaller but growing fast — has never been party to a bilateral arms-control treaty at all. Mr Trump has long argued that any future deal must include Beijing, and the Shenzhen session is the first time the three leaders will test that idea face to face. The president is scheduled to meet Mr Xi separately on Thursday afternoon, for talks and a working lunch, before the three-way gathering at the end of his China visit, which follows a stop in Manila earlier in the week.

The announcement itself was a study in how far the three sides have yet to travel. Beijing and Moscow had not formally confirmed their participation when the White House announcement landed on Friday, and the planning was described as still in motion, with no format or venue details released. The agenda is still being negotiated in public: Washington wants nuclear arms and Iran on the table and Ukraine kept off it, while the other two capitals have said little about what they expect from the room. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has already voiced concern that the meeting could become a platform for discussing agreements on Ukraine without Kyiv present — a worry the White House's insistence that Ukraine is off the agenda has done only partly to calm.

Analysts say the meeting's significance lies less in what it might decide than in the fact of it happening at all. A three-way summit of the leaders of the United States, China and Russia has no precedent, and its staging — at an APEC summit of 21 Pacific economies that China is hosting under the theme "Building an Asia-Pacific Community to Prosper Together" — guarantees it will overshadow the trade gathering itself. The APEC CEO Summit runs on 17 and 18 November, ahead of the leaders' meeting on 18 and 19 November, but the world's attention will be on the room in Shenzhen on the Thursday. On Polymarket, traders have taken notice: the market asking whether Trump, Putin and Xi will be seen together before 2027 rose 11.5 per cent this week, and traders put the chance of Mr Trump meeting Mr Putin by 31 December at 96 per cent.

What happens next depends on whether the first conversation survives contact with the three leaders' very different purposes. For Mr Trump, the meeting is a chance to claim progress on the nuclear file he has made a signature ambition; for Mr Xi, hosting the gathering burnishes China's standing as the convening power of the Pacific; for Mr Putin, a seat at the table with the other two nuclear giants is itself a prize. The risk is that a meeting announced with no agreement expected, no agenda agreed and two of the three participants yet to confirm it becomes a photograph rather than a process. The test will come on Thursday, when the three men walk into the room — and in the days after, when it becomes clear whether anyone intends to walk back in.
