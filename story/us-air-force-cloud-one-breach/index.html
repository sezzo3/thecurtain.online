Hackers Breach the US Air Force's Cloud One Platform, Putting Sensitive Military Data at Risk

Sezgin Baktiaya · 2026-11-27 · 5 min read

Hackers have breached Cloud One, the US Air Force's flagship cloud computing platform, three people familiar with the matter told Reuters, in a development that could jeopardise a vast swathe of sensitive tactical, personnel and logistics data. The intrusion was discovered within the past month during work to upgrade the system, and Reuters could not establish who the hackers were, the full scope of the intrusion, or whether any data had been stolen.

One source said the intruders exploited a software vulnerability to reach the platform's control plane — the layer that manages the cloud itself — likening it to a "universal remote" for the Air Force's computing. With that access, the intruders could in theory have checked the operational status of military aircraft or tracked the movement of spare parts, although it is unclear whether they did so. Reuters could not identify the specific vulnerability the hackers used.

Cloud One was launched in 2017 as a one-stop shop for the Air Force's IT needs, combining the resources of four operators — Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet — to power scores of applications. Among them are systems that manage personnel data and pay, logistics and maintenance, as well as the Cloud-Based Command and Control system, which collates air defence radar and other data feeds from across North America, and the Theater Integrated Combat Munitions System, which tracks bombs and missiles across more than 200 US Air Force locations around the world. Two of the sources said Cloud One is among the US military's biggest cloud platforms. "A breach here is a big deal," one of them said.

SAIC was the prime contractor for Cloud One until 2025, when Leidos took over. SAIC did not respond to a request for comment, while Leidos referred questions to the Air Force. The Air Force told Reuters it "does not comment on the security posture, vulnerabilities or operational status of specific networks and systems", adding: "We continuously take prudent measures to protect Department (of the Air Force) information, systems and mission capabilities." Investigators have called in US Cyber Command to assess the damage; Cyber Command did not respond to requests for comment.

The discovery has already caused disruption across the platform. Efforts to migrate back-office applications to Cloud One, a longstanding military priority, have been paused, and updates to applications already running on the platform have been blocked, leaving Air Force personnel unable to apply fixes just as a new batch of serious software vulnerabilities has been disclosed across the technology industry. Some users have also had their administrator rights revoked. All five sources spoke to Reuters on condition of anonymity.

The breach comes amid a string of recently disclosed cyber incidents across the US government, including two at the FBI, a hack of Pentagon data, a leak of Department of Homeland Security personnel information, a breach affecting at least two spy agencies and a hack at the Treasury Department. It also comes amid heightened anxiety over AI-powered intrusions, which are a particular problem for cloud-based networks because AI models can swiftly detect weaknesses across wide surface areas. Cloud One has long been in hackers' crosshairs: in a 2023 commentary, its leadership boasted of a "100% success rate" in beating back a "staggering" number of penetration attempts. "There is no room for error when it comes to security," they wrote.
