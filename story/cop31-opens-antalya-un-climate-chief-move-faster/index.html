COP31 Opens in Antalya as UN Climate Chief Warns the World Must 'Move Faster'

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-09 · 3 min read

The 31st United Nations climate conference opened in Antalya, Türkiye, on Monday, as representatives of nearly 200 countries gathered for two weeks of talks aimed at turning years of climate pledges into concrete action. The summit, known as COP31, runs until 20 November at the Antalya Expo Center on Türkiye's Mediterranean coast, and it is the first COP since the United States left the Paris Agreement earlier this year. A World Leaders' Summit, bringing heads of state and government to Antalya, is scheduled for 11 and 12 November.

Türkiye holds the rotating COP presidency, with Environment Minister Murat Kurum serving as COP31 president, while Australia's Climate Change and Energy Minister, Chris Bowen, presides over the negotiations — a co-presidency arrangement agreed after both countries bid to host the summit. The conference's overarching theme is "Towards a Sustainable, Resilient and Equitable Future", and its organisers have billed it as a "COP of implementation", focused less on new headline promises and more on verified emissions cuts, finance flows and grid upgrades. The first thematic day on Monday centred on food security, agriculture and public health under climate stress.

Opening the conference, UN Climate Change Executive Secretary Simon Stiell urged governments to accelerate, saying: "The urgency of this moment must drive us forward", and adding that progress so far was "nowhere near fast enough". His warning was backed by the United Nations Environment Programme's 2026 Emissions Gap Report, published in October, which estimated that even fully implemented national pledges would still produce roughly 2.6°C of warming this century — far above the Paris Agreement's 1.5°C aspiration. The summit opens against a backdrop of another year of record heat and flood damage, war-driven energy security fears, and surging electricity demand from artificial intelligence.

Climate finance is set to be one of the hardest fights in Antalya. At COP29 in Baku, countries agreed a new collective goal of at least $300 billion a year by 2035 for developing countries, with developed countries taking the lead, and delegates in Antalya will now argue over how that money is raised and delivered. Türkiye's presidency has put electrification at the centre of the agenda, with a goal of electrifying more than a third of the global economy by 2035, up from around a fifth today. Adaptation — helping vulnerable countries cope with impacts already under way — is also high on the list, with the Food and Agriculture Organization warning that extreme heat worsened by El Niño is making food production increasingly difficult.

The mood outside the negotiating halls is sceptical in places. On Reddit, where COP31 is being followed in r/climate, one widely noted comment in a live discussion thread argued that with "zero actual results" expected, the gathering "should be a Zoom call instead" (r/climate). That scepticism reflects a wider frustration among climate campaigners: after three decades of COP meetings, the gap between what governments promise and what they deliver remains the central fault line, and this year's test is whether Antalya produces measurable delivery rather than another round of declarations.

What happens next will be decided first by the leaders arriving on Wednesday. The World Leaders' Summit on 11 and 12 November is expected to set the political ceiling for what ministers can agree in the second week, before the conference closes on 20 November. With the United States absent from the Paris framework, the talks will also test whether other major emitters — above all China, the European Union and India — are willing to carry the process forward without Washington at the table.
