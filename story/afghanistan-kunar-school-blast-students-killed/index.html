Explosion Beside School in Afghanistan's Kunar Kills Seven Students, Injures Nearly 100

Sezgin Baktiaya · 2026-11-28 · 5 min read

An explosion beside a middle school in the Andar Ser area of Asadabad, the capital of Afghanistan's eastern Kunar province, killed seven students and injured nearly 100 others on Saturday morning. The blast struck at about 10am local time (05:30 GMT) in a house next to the school, while pupils were at school. The head of Kunar Provincial Hospital, Mawla Dad, said the casualty figures were preliminary and could change as more information came in; the Associated Press put the toll at seven dead and more than 90 wounded, while CNN reported up to 99 injured.

Officials said the explosion came from dynamite stored in the house, which belonged to people involved in mining. A spokesperson for the Taliban Interior Ministry, Abdul Mateen Qani, said a preliminary investigation found the blast originated at a depot storing explosives belonging to mining companies near the school. The province's director of information and culture, Mawlawi Sami-ul-Haq Bayan, said the house was used by mining workers who had kept dynamite on the premises. The initial findings point to negligence at the depot, according to the Associated Press — but what set the explosives off has not been established, and officials stressed that the inquiry was still under way.

The injured were taken to Kunar Provincial Hospital, and some to Nangarhar Regional Hospital, while several nearby homes were damaged in the blast, according to local reports. Up to 15 of the wounded remained in hospital, while others had been discharged, the Associated Press reported. Kunar's police spokesperson, Farid Dehqan, confirmed that several students had been killed or wounded and said provincial investigation teams had reached the site.

No group claimed responsibility, and nothing in the official accounts so far suggests a deliberate attack. Al Jazeera, citing the hospital director, reported at least six students killed and 97 injured — a reminder that the toll is still provisional and that the counts differ between outlets because they were given at different stages of a rescue and treatment effort that was still unfolding. Authorities said they would release further details once the investigation was complete.

Kunar, a mountainous province on Afghanistan's border with Pakistan, has a long history of small-scale mining, and explosives stored informally near homes and workplaces are a recurring hazard. The blast is the second time in months that a school has been the scene of a mass-casualty incident involving explosives: in August, a hand grenade attack at a school complex in Kabul wounded more than 40 children aged between 9 and 14. Saturday's explosion, though apparently accidental, underlined how exposed Afghan schoolchildren remain — to war, to negligence, and to the everyday dangers of a country awash with explosives.

For the families in Andar Ser, the wait for news continued into the afternoon as hospitals treated the wounded and investigators picked through the wreckage of the house. The school itself, and the question of how a depot full of dynamite came to sit beside a classroom, will now be at the centre of the official inquiry. Until it reports, the one fact no official disputed is the simplest: seven children went to school on Saturday morning and did not come home.
