Russian Military Plane Crashes in Siberia, Killing All 19 on Board

Sezgin Baktiaya · 2026-11-17 · 5 min read

A Russian military transport plane crashed in Siberia on Tuesday, killing all 19 people on board, Russia's Defence Ministry said, in one of the deadliest Russian military aviation accidents in years. The Antonov An-12 went down during a scheduled flight in an uninhabited area of the Kemerovo region, the ministry said in a statement, adding that a technical malfunction was the preliminary cause.

The ministry said there were 19 people on board, including the crew, and that all of them had died. Earlier Russian media reports had put the death toll at 17; the ministry's statement revised the figure upward. The aircraft, an An-12BK, was flying from Tolmachevo Airport in Novosibirsk to Kansk Air Base in Krasnoyarsk Krai, carrying crew members and soldiers as well as spare parts.

The crash happened near the village of Yagunovo, south of the regional capital Kemerovo. According to reports, the aircraft caught fire in flight — the Russian news channel Baza reported that a fuel leak had started a fire in one of the engines — and the crew declared an emergency and steered the burning plane away from residential areas before it came down at around 9:30 p.m. local time. Wreckage was scattered across a radius of roughly 500 metres, and no homes were damaged.

Kemerovo Governor Ilya Seredyuk said an emergency response team had quickly extinguished the blaze and police had sealed off the area. Seredyuk said he had travelled to the scene and that emergency services were working there, with the region providing the necessary assistance. Troops from the National Guard were also deployed to the crash site.

The Defence Ministry said an investigation was under way, with a military commission from the Russian Air Force taking part. The An-12 is a Soviet-era four-engine turboprop transport that has been in service since the late 1950s and remains a workhorse of Russian military aviation; the type has been involved in a series of fatal accidents over the decades, including crashes in Russia's far east and Siberia.

The crash site is more than 2,000 miles from the fighting in Ukraine, and there is no suggestion of hostile action. Russian officials have stressed that the flight was routine, but the loss of all 19 people on board makes it one of the worst peacetime accidents involving a Russian military transport aircraft in recent years. Further details, including the identities of those killed, are expected as the investigation continues.
