Russia Pounds Kyiv With Ballistic Missiles Days Before Abu Dhabi Peace Talks

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-08 · 2 min read

Russia fired 36 ballistic missiles and 172 drones at Kyiv and the surrounding region overnight, one of the heaviest single barrages on the Ukrainian capital in recent months, just three days before negotiators from Ukraine, Russia and the United States are due to meet in Abu Dhabi for a new round of peace talks.

The attack struck homes and commercial buildings, damaged energy infrastructure and forced officials to close the city's 816-metre Northern Bridge, a major route across the Dnipro River linking the capital with large residential areas on the east bank. A kindergarten was among the buildings hit, and power, water and heating supplies were disrupted across parts of the city as temperatures fell towards freezing. At least nine people were wounded, including a child, according to Ukrainian officials, while Reuters reported at least two people killed and around a dozen wounded nationwide.

Ukraine's air force said it shot down 149 of the 172 drones, but only four of the 36 ballistic missiles — a gap that underlines how little capacity Kyiv has against ballistic weapons on this scale. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy blamed the shortfall on a lack of US-made Patriot interceptors, and said some of the missiles fired were KN-23 ballistic missiles manufactured and supplied by North Korea, a claim based on preliminary findings from impact sites that has not yet been independently confirmed.

Russia's defence ministry confirmed the strike, saying it had targeted the Northern Bridge — which Moscow claims is used to transport weapons and military equipment — and the Meridian plant in Kyiv, which Russia says produces Flamingo cruise missiles. Zelenskyy said Russia was preparing for the talks by "launching ballistic missiles at Ukraine's critical infrastructure", and Ukrainian intelligence has warned that Moscow is preparing further large-scale attacks on the country's energy and logistics networks.

The barrage came as the death toll from one of the deadliest strikes on civilians this year continued to rise. Emergency services said the toll from Monday's Russian strike on an aid distribution point in a school building in Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region, had risen to 55, after more bodies were recovered from the rubble. The United Nations human rights mission in Ukraine has said the bombing likely violated international law.

The talks in Abu Dhabi, scheduled for 10 and 11 November, will be the fourth round brokered by the Trump administration and the first trilateral engagement of its kind since February. US special envoy Steve Witkoff has said the working-level discussions will focus on post-war security arrangements, de-escalation, humanitarian measures and steps towards a peace agreement, and President Donald Trump said on Thursday that a deal may be imminent after meeting Kirill Dmitriev, President Vladimir Putin's special envoy, at the White House. Moscow, meanwhile, has dashed hopes of European participation: foreign ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova said on Saturday that European countries have no role at the negotiating table, and warned that any foreign military presence in Ukraine would be treated as a legitimate target. Prediction markets remain strikingly confident the talks will go ahead, with Polymarket pricing Russia-Ukraine peace talks by 3 December at 97%.
