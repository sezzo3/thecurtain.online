Trump, Xi and Putin Sign Joint 'Peace Declaration' in Shenzhen — With No Word on Ukraine

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-19 · 5 min read

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin signed a joint "peace declaration" on Thursday after the first trilateral summit of the world's three largest nuclear powers, pledging that a nuclear war "cannot be won and must never be fought" — but the four-point document says nothing about the war in Ukraine. The 90-minute meeting at Kylin Villa in Shenzhen, held on the sidelines of the APEC summit, ended with the leaders issuing a statement their own officials had said not to expect: hours earlier, a senior American official briefing reporters had been blunt — "We do not expect to get to any kind of agreement."

The declaration, published by China's Xinhua news agency and the Kremlin under the title "U.S.-China-Russia Summit Peace Declaration", commits the three permanent members of the UN Security Council to reforming the United Nations "so that the UN can respond more effectively to global challenges", and to increasing the production and supply of refined petroleum products "for the benefit of all nations". It also states that the principle of state consent and respect for the immunities of state officials are "of paramount importance", and that the three countries will conduct a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing the proliferation of nuclear weapons.

The document's most striking feature is what it leaves out. Ukraine, the war that has defined Mr Putin's relations with the West since 2022 and that Mr Trump had said he would raise, is not mentioned at all. Neither is the war between the United States and Iran, which has spread across the Gulf this week, nor any timetable for the nuclear dialogue the leaders promised. The declaration contains no disarmament numbers, no deadlines and no ceasefire — only pledges to keep talking, to reform the UN and to keep oil flowing.

The immunity clause is the passage that will draw the closest reading. It says the International Criminal Court and similar bodies must respect the immunities of state officials — language widely read as aimed at the arrest warrant the ICC issued for Mr Putin after the full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Mr Putin has avoided travel to countries that might enforce it, and the declaration's wording amounts to the three leaders jointly questioning the court's reach. The Times reported that Mr Putin, asked afterwards whether there would be peace, replied: "Only God knows."

The summit itself was a study in lowered expectations exceeded. The three leaders sat at a round table in a villa among wooded hills beside a lake, described the meeting as historic, and then made no public statements at all before Mr Trump departed for the United States. The meeting came after New START, the last treaty capping American and Russian strategic weapons, expired in February, leaving the two largest nuclear powers without any binding limit on their arsenals for the first time in decades — and with China, whose arsenal is far smaller, repeatedly rejecting three-way disarmament talks as "neither fair, reasonable nor realistic".

What happens next will be measured in the bilateral tracks the summit was supposed to open. Mr Trump held a separate bilateral meeting and working lunch with Mr Xi to test a trade truce reached earlier this month, and a one-on-one with Mr Putin was planned for Thursday, where Ukraine was expected to feature. Smaller countries have already voiced unease that three great powers met to discuss the world's security without them. For now, the first trilateral summit of the nuclear age has produced a declaration that promises peace in general terms — and leaves the two wars that dominate the world's headlines untouched.
