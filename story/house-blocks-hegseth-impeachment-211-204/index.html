House Shelves Hegseth Impeachment in 211–204 Vote, but Democrats Can Revive It in January

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-11 · 4 min read

The United States House of Representatives has voted 211-204 to send eight articles of impeachment against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, a procedural move that effectively kills the effort for the remainder of this Congress. The vote came on Tuesday evening, after the resolution's author, Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky, forced the chamber to act by filing it as a privileged matter, which required a decision within two legislative days. Massie was the only Republican to vote against the referral, joining all 203 Democrats who voted in opposing it; 210 Republicans and one independent, Kevin Kiley of California, voted to send the articles to committee, and 18 members did not vote.

The eight articles, contained in a 34-page resolution filed on Monday, accused Hegseth of high crimes and misdemeanours across the administration's military operations of the past year. Three of the articles charged him with waging war against Iran without congressional authorisation, in violation of the War Powers Resolution; others accused him of ignoring laws meant to limit civilian deaths, directing extrajudicial killings in the Pentagon's lethal strikes on suspected drug boats in the Caribbean, directing the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, carrying out strikes in Yemen without proper authorisation, and retaliating against Senator Mark Kelly. Massie read the resolution aloud on the House floor for about an hour and twenty minutes on Monday, and the House never debated or voted on the merits of any of the charges.

The vote itself was a study in procedural warfare rather than a judgement on the allegations. Representative Darrell Issa moved to refer the resolution, known as H.Res. 1655, to the Judiciary Committee, and the motion was agreed to as Roll Call 317 at 6:02 p.m. Eastern. Because the referral passed, the two-day clock Massie had started expired without a floor vote on the articles themselves, and the committee is under no obligation to take them up. Eleven Democrats and seven Republicans were absent, including former Speaker Nancy Pelosi, and the seven-vote margin was smaller than the number of Democrats who missed the vote — a detail that underscored how narrowly the Republican leadership's manoeuvre succeeded.

Reaction split along familiar lines. President Donald Trump called the result an exoneration, while Speaker Mike Johnson had argued earlier in the day that impeaching a Cabinet secretary "in the middle of hostilities abroad would be a terrible, terrible look". Massie, who said on Monday that he would not bring another impeachment resolution, told reporters after the vote that he was disappointed but not surprised, adding that his colleagues knew what to do but "just can't bring themselves to do it". He said instead that he planned to force a House vote on a separate war powers resolution concerning Iran, keeping the underlying constitutional argument alive even as the impeachment vehicle itself was parked.

The vote also closed a chapter on Massie's own career in Congress. The Kentucky Republican, who first introduced a nearly identical impeachment resolution on 15 September only for Johnson to adjourn the House before a vote could take place, lost his primary election in May to a Trump-backed challenger and leaves Congress in early January. On the same day as the vote, House Republicans chose Jim Jordan, who chairs the Judiciary Committee where the articles now sit, as their leader for the next Congress — a reminder that the committee's inaction is a choice its own chairman will control.

What happens next is a matter of the calendar. The current Congress expires on 3 January, when the articles will lapse if the committee takes no action, and the Democrats who won control of both the House and the Senate in the 3 November midterm elections will hold the majorities needed to revive proceedings. Hakeem Jeffries is expected to become Speaker, and Democrats have said they will take up the matter when they take power — meaning Tuesday's vote is likely a pause, not a verdict, and Hegseth's tenure will be relitigated in a very different House within eight weeks. Online, the reaction was swift and partisan: a r/politics thread on the vote passed 10,000 upvotes, with the most-upvoted replies noting that Democrats could simply file fresh articles in January, while a second thread — on House Democrats blaming traffic for missing the vote — drew nearly 8,000 upvotes, its top reply questioning the excuse.
