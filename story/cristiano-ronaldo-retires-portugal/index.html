The End of an Era: Cristiano Ronaldo Hands Back Portugal's No. 7 Shirt

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-06 · 4 min read

Cristiano Ronaldo announced on Thursday that he is retiring from international football, closing a 23-year career with Portugal that made him the most capped men's player and the highest scorer in the history of international football. In a five-minute video message posted to his social media accounts, the 41-year-old told the Portuguese people that "the day has come for me to hand back the No. 7 shirt of our national team". He added: "Today the time has come to pass it on to the future. It was not an easy decision because some decisions are not made with the mind alone. They are also made with the heart. A journey that began in August 2003 and changed my life forever has come to an end."

The numbers attached to that journey are unlikely ever to be matched. Ronaldo made 234 appearances for Portugal and scored 146 goals, both men's world records, and scored for his country in 23 consecutive calendar years. He played in a record six European Championships and a joint-record six World Cups, won the European Championship in 2016 — Portugal's first major trophy — and lifted the Nations League in 2019 and again in 2025. His final appearance came on 24 September, in a 1-0 Nations League win over Wales. He will continue playing club football with Al-Nassr in Saudi Arabia, where he is expected to finish the season, and he remains in pursuit of 1,000 career goals, currently sitting on 985.

Yet the ending was not the farewell many had imagined. Ronaldo's last weeks with the national team were shaped by a bitter falling-out with head coach Jorge Jesus. After being left on the bench for a 2-1 win over Norway, Ronaldo walked out of the Portugal camp in Copenhagen on 30 September, before a Nations League match against Denmark, and was handed a 30-day suspension and a €255 fine by the Portuguese Football Federation. Reports from Portugal said he had demanded a public apology from the coach over broken promises about his playing time. The federation has since paid tribute to its "eternal captain" and promised to organise an event in his honour, but the manner of the departure — a video of a man reading a letter alone, rather than a farewell match in a packed stadium — has dominated much of the reaction.

That contrast was the thread running through the response of fans and former players. On Reddit, a thread in r/soccer sharing his farewell message drew more than 13,700 upvotes and nearly 2,000 comments, with the most upvoted response reading: "he couldve ended it off in a much better way, shame. Still an insane career." Others, including self-described Lionel Messi fans, wrote that the announcement marked the end of the era they grew up with — the schoolyard arguments over who was better, Messi or Ronaldo, and the legendary moments the two produced across two decades. Several noted that Ronaldo never really had a proper farewell anywhere, leaving Real Madrid abruptly too, while others joked that if Portugal were to win the World Cup in 2030, his timing would look very different. Prediction markets had seen it coming: Polymarket traders had priced the chance of Ronaldo announcing his retirement in 2026 at 95 per cent.

The timing also closes a chapter that opened only last month, when Messi played his own much-anticipated farewell match for Argentina in a stadium full of celebrations. Ronaldo's exit, by contrast, has the feel of a door closing quietly after an argument — a reminder that even the most decorated careers do not always end on their own terms. Portugal, coached by Jorge Jesus, finish their Nations League group with games against Denmark in Braga on 14 November and away to Wales in Cardiff three days later, and will now do so without the player who defined the national team for a generation. The No. 7 shirt, Ronaldo said, "will remain. The pride of having worn it will be mine forever. Portugal will always be a part of me. Farewell, until we meet again."

What comes next for Ronaldo is the final stretch of his club career and the long chase for 1,000 goals, a milestone no player has reached in official matches. For Portugal, the question is who inherits the shirt and the weight that comes with it. The team that won Euro 2016 and two Nations League titles was built, in the public imagination at least, around one man; the generation that follows — the players who grew up watching Ronaldo — must now prove that Portuguese football is bigger than its greatest ever player. The tributes will keep coming, the federation's promised ceremony included, but the era itself ended on Thursday, with a video, a letter, and a shirt handed back.
