Eleven Days After His Fall, Jensen Huang Is Back at Nvidia — With the White House Behind Him

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-24 · 5 min read

Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and board director on Monday morning, just eleven days after he resigned in disgrace, in a reversal driven by an extraordinary pressure campaign from the White House. The company said it had acted after receiving "input from senior levels of the United States government" and was confident his return posed no national security risk. Investors roared their approval: Nvidia shares surged 31.5 percent, their biggest daily percentage gain in 23 years, restoring nearly $1 trillion in market value, while the Nasdaq jumped 6 percent and the S&P 500 rose 4.7 percent. Asian markets followed on Tuesday, with Japan's Nikkei up 5 percent, South Korea's KOSPI up 7.4 percent and Taiwanese shares up 9 percent.

Huang's fall had been dizzyingly fast. He resigned on 12 November after revelations of extramarital affairs, including a lengthy relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud computing company that buys Nvidia's chips and that United States officials suspect of ties to Chinese intelligence. The board had unanimously accepted his resignation, told investors he would not return, promoted chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim chief executive and begun searching for a permanent successor, reportedly reaching out to Rene Haas, the chief executive of Arm. President Donald Trump had at first suggested the company hire former Apple chief executive Tim Cook instead.

What changed was a frantic 48 hours of lobbying from the top of the American government. Trump, who had been stewing over a deepening stock market sell-off, decided late last week that only Huang's return could stop the slide, and turned to Vice President JD Vance to get it done. Over the weekend, Attorney General Todd Blanche told Trump and Vance that Huang was neither the target nor the subject of any Justice Department investigation and that the department had no evidence to suggest he had broken laws or was a national security risk. Trump and Vance then relayed those assurances to board members, and Trump himself called the board directly. "I've been speaking to the board directly and indirectly, and I felt very strongly about it," Trump said on CNBC on Monday. "I think we have one of our great stars back in play." Board chairman Harvey Jones said simply: "Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA."

The market reaction was historic. The Wall Street Journal reported that Nvidia notched the largest one-day market capitalisation gain on record for an American company, recovering much of the roughly 30 percent slide since Huang's resignation, a fall that had dragged down the broader market and fuelled fears of contagion across the artificial intelligence trade. Short sellers were facing about $12.2 billion in paper losses on Monday, their biggest one-day hit since at least 2019, according to market data firm ORTEX. Huang returned to Nvidia's Silicon Valley headquarters on Monday to cheers from staff who chanted his name, and on Reddit the reinstatement thread drew more than 9,100 upvotes in r/stocks and 15,900 in r/technology, while a Polymarket market on Huang returning as chief executive and Nvidia ending 2026 above $4 trillion in value rose 62.2 percent on the day.

Yet the scandal itself is far from closed. On the same day Huang was reinstated, the Commerce Department placed Megaspeed and its affiliates on its trade blacklist, barring American companies from shipping goods, software or technology to them without a licence. The affiliates span Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand, and the department said they had been found to be acting contrary to the national security or foreign policy interests of the United States. The Justice Department is still investigating whether Megaspeed broke federal export control laws by trading Nvidia chips to China, and its Bureau of Industry and Security has been probing the firm since 2024, interviewing Alice Huang in June 2025, the same month Nvidia put Megaspeed on its own "no sell" list. Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren called the reinstatement "reckless" and said the board still owed Congress answers about its compliance with export controls.

Huang's exoneration also came before Nvidia had even begun in earnest its own investigation into the allegations against him, according to Reuters, and questions remain about what the board knew and when. He is due to testify before Congress on 3 December, and lawmakers are discussing fast-tracking a chip export control bill next month. For now, though, the man who built Nvidia from a video game chipmaker into the most important company in the artificial intelligence economy is back in charge, with the president's blessing, and the market has decided that is what matters most. Whether the political price of that rescue becomes clear only in the hearings ahead.
