Britain Commits £10 Billion in Fresh Support for Ukraine as Winter Nears

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-10-29 · 4 min read

Britain announced a further package of more than £10 billion in economic and military support for Ukraine on Wednesday, 28 October 2026, as Kyiv prepares for another winter of Russia’s full-scale war. Chancellor John Healey set out the commitment in the government’s Budget, describing a mix of financing, export support and urgent military assistance.

Healey told MPs the package would be “based on loans, export finance guarantees and £500 million in urgent military funding for this winter.” Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Healey had spoken with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy before the announcement. Healey said Zelenskyy told them: “you’ve made my day.”

The Treasury says the economic component will make £7 billion available over three years. This includes up to £5.5 billion in World Bank loan guarantees and the use of the World Bank’s International Development Association crisis facility to mobilise a further £1.5 billion for Ukraine’s recovery. These are financing commitments, not £7 billion in immediate cash grants.

A further £3 billion will increase the capacity of UK Export Finance for Ukraine, prioritised for military support and taking the total cover limit to £6.5 billion. The package also includes £500 million in additional military funding for Ukraine’s air defences this winter, intended to address urgent gaps as Russian attacks continue to target critical infrastructure.

The timing is significant. Ukraine’s air defences face heightened pressure during winter, when attacks on energy infrastructure can leave homes and essential services exposed. Kyiv has repeatedly urged allies to speed up deliveries of interceptors and other air-defence systems.

London framed the announcement as a multi-year commitment to help sustain Ukraine’s public finances and defence, alongside the UK’s existing pledge of £3 billion a year in military support until 2030–31 or for as long as needed. The new package combines direct military funding with guarantees and export-credit capacity, whose eventual value depends on how the financing is taken up.

For Ukraine, the immediate test will be whether the winter air-defence funding can help meet urgent needs in time. The broader test is whether the economic and export-finance commitments can support the country through the longer work of maintaining public services, defending itself and rebuilding.
