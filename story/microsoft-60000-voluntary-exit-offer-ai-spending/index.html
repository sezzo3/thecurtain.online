The Company Spending $175 Billion on AI Just Offered 60,000 People a Way Out

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-10-26 · 5 min read

On Monday, 26 October, Microsoft announced a new Voluntary Exit Offer for eligible employees in participating organisations. The company says the programme gives people considering a future outside Microsoft another option, with financial support and career-planning resources.

About 60,000 employees—roughly 27% of Microsoft’s global workforce—are eligible, according to reporting confirmed by the company. That is the size of the potential pool, not a layoff count. How many people accept the offer remains unknown.

Advertisement

Chief People Officer Amy Coleman said the offer is available to eligible employees in participating teams in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and India at internal level 70 or below. Not every organisation is taking part; Microsoft says participation depends on each business’s priorities, customer commitments and workforce needs.

Microsoft has not published a single company-wide target for departures in its announcement. Eligible employees are being sent individual offer details, so terms and timing may depend on the person and their location. The offer is voluntary and should not be confused with a notice that 60,000 jobs are being eliminated.

The announcement comes as Microsoft commits extraordinary sums to infrastructure supporting cloud and AI services. In its fiscal 2026 fourth-quarter earnings call, the company said its calendar-year 2026 capital-expenditure expectation was approximately $175 billion after an accounting reclassification of data-centre leases. Microsoft’s annual report says it added 88 data centres during fiscal 2026.

The contrast is striking: a company expanding the physical infrastructure behind its AI ambitions is also offering a large group of employees a supported exit. Microsoft describes the programme as a way for participating teams to adapt to changing business needs; it has not said the offer is a direct consequence of AI spending.

The offer also follows a voluntary retirement programme earlier in 2026. Microsoft said the new exit offer builds on what it learned from that programme, while its public note does not specify how many employees it expects to accept this time.

For employees who receive the offer, the decision is personal: remain at Microsoft through another period of change, or consider leaving with support. For the industry, the programme is another sign that the AI build-out is changing not only budgets and data-centre footprints, but workforce choices too.
