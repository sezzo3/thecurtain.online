After the Midterm Rout, Trump Doubles Down: Stephen Miller to Run the White House

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-07 · 4 min read

President Donald Trump has replaced his chief of staff, Susie Wiles, with longtime adviser Stephen Miller, in the first major shake-up of his administration since Republicans lost control of both houses of Congress in Tuesday's midterm elections. Trump announced the change on Friday on Truth Social, saying Miller would take over the post on Monday, November 9. Wiles, the first woman ever to serve as White House chief of staff, will move to the Trump Political Operation.

The departure had been building for barely a day. Wiles submitted her resignation on Thursday evening, hours after the scale of the Republican defeat became clear. Democrats flipped at least seven Senate seats and retook the House, ending two years of unified Republican rule in Washington. Trump had publicly dismissed speculation about Wiles leaving as recently as October, saying she would remain in the job "for a long time". She was also his longest-serving chief of staff, a role that has consumed five occupants across his two terms.

Miller is one of the most uncompromising figures in Trump's orbit and the central architect of the administration's hard-line immigration policies, including the travel bans and family separations of the first term. His promotion places the author of the policies that defined the administration's domestic agenda at the centre of the White House operation, at the very moment voters delivered a verdict against that agenda. Allies including Steve Bannon had reportedly been pushing for changes to the president's senior team.

Trump himself signalled that more heads could roll. In an interview with CBS's "60 Minutes", recorded on Friday morning and due to air in full on Sunday, he was asked whether he planned to fire anyone after the midterm losses. "I do, yeah. A little bit," he replied, adding: "I love my Cabinet. I think they're good. But I'll be doing maybe a few changes." He declined to name anyone, but made clear he had no plans to remove War Secretary Pete Hegseth, who faces a possible impeachment vote when the House reconvenes next week.

Speaking to reporters before departing for Florida on Friday, Trump praised Miller's political understanding and management skills, but rejected any suggestion that the decision was prompted by the election result. "Not because of the election. It's just time," he said. "The election wasn't my election. I wasn't on the ballot." The White House mood, meanwhile, has been described in reporting as defiant, with the president insisting the defeat belonged to the party rather than to him.

The move caps a year of churn at the top of the administration, which has already seen the departures of Attorney General Pam Bondi, Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem and Labor Secretary Lori Chavez-DeRemer. The question now is whether Miller's appointment is a one-off or the first step in a wider purge — and whether a president facing a Democratic Congress for his final two years intends to govern around it rather than with it. Online, the reaction was swift and overwhelmingly critical: a Reddit thread on the announcement in r/politics drew more than 16,000 upvotes and 2,800 comments, with the top-rated comment reading the appointment as Trump doubling down after the defeat, and even threads in the conservative-leaning r/Conservative community were sceptical.
