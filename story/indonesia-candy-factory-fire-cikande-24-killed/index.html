Twenty-Four Dead and Dozens Still Missing After Fire Guts Indonesian Candy Factory

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-23 · 5 min read

Indonesia confirmed on Monday that 24 people have died in a fire that tore through a candy factory at the weekend, as rescuers picked through unstable wreckage in the search for about 30 workers still missing. The blaze broke out on Saturday evening in one building of the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia confectionery factory in Cikande, in Serang regency in Banten province, about an hour's drive west of Jakarta, and burned through the night before firefighters brought it under control on Sunday. It is one of the deadliest industrial fires in Indonesia in years, and the toll is expected to rise as the search continues.

The fire struck during the Saturday shift, when around 300 people were present at the factory complex, according to the company's chief executive, Steven Erwin Wijaya. Banten's governor, Andra Soni, said about 60 people were inside the building when the fire started, and television pictures showed flames engulfing the three-storey structure and sending thick black smoke into the sky. By Monday, 29 people had been confirmed safe, five of whom were in hospital, but the search of the gutted building was still under way, hampered by remaining hotspots and a structure that rescue officials said was growing more unstable by the hour. "The search continued on Monday with remaining hotspots and the building's increasingly unstable structure posing safety risks for rescuers," said Al Amrad, head of the Banten search and rescue agency.

The recovery effort has been grim. Rescuers had taken away 24 body bags from the rubble by Monday, but most contained only individual human remains, and the authorities on the ground could not immediately confirm the death toll from the bags alone. The intensity of the fire meant the victims could not be identified by sight, and police official Wahyu Hidayati said efforts were under way to collect DNA samples from relatives — a process that could take weeks. Outside the factory gate, relatives of the missing gathered and waited for news, while ambulances came and went from a command post set up at the site. The number of missing has shifted as the count has been reconciled: early reports put it at 36, and police at one point said around 50 employees were unaccounted for, before the figure settled at about 30 on Monday.

The factory is owned by PT United Family Food, a company that employs about 5,000 people and supplies the Indonesian domestic market with brands including Milkita, a popular milk-based candy, and Lemonilo instant noodles. Milkita is exported to more than 40 countries, and the factory is one of the company's main production sites. The cause of the fire is still under investigation, but preliminary reports indicated it may have been caused by a short circuit in an air conditioner, according to Said Iqbal, a presidential adviser on labour issues. Mr Said told reporters that 24 people had died, that the implementation of safety protocols at the factory was being assessed, and that he would summon the factory's chief executive.

What happens next will be measured in the identification of the dead and the findings of the investigation. The DNA process alone could take weeks, leaving families in limbo, and the questions about how a fire could spread so fast through a building holding dozens of workers — and whether safety rules were followed — will now be put to the company.

For now, the search teams in Cikande are still working through the wreckage, and the confirmed toll of 24 is, by the authorities' own account, not yet final.
