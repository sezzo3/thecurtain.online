Nine Killed as Business Jet Crashes in South Florida Neighbourhood

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-02 · 3 min read

A business jet carrying nine people nose-dived into a South Florida neighbourhood on Sunday afternoon and erupted into a giant fireball, killing everyone on board and forcing about 600 residents of a nearby mobile home community from their homes. The crash, in Palm Beach County near Greenacres and Cresthaven, left a scorched roadway and a large crater beside a busy road — and an investigation that, as of Sunday night, had established the scale of the loss but not its cause.

The aircraft was a Learjet 60, registration N722BD, which had departed Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas and was bound for North Eleuthera Airport in the Bahamas, according to the Federal Aviation Administration. It crashed at about 12:50 p.m. Eastern time on Sunday, 1 November, near the intersection of 10th Avenue North and Military Trail, the Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office said. “Today's plane crash in Palm Beach County, Florida is a horrific tragedy,” Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said in a post on X, confirming that all nine people on board had been killed. The FAA said its information was preliminary and subject to change, and officials had not publicly named the people on board as of Sunday night.

Witnesses and officials described a sudden, catastrophic descent. Flight-tracking data showed the aircraft descending tens of thousands of feet in about a minute before tracking cut off, according to reports citing ADS-B data; one account put the final descent rate at more than 30,000 feet per minute. Air traffic control recordings captured by LiveATC.net and reported by the Associated Press show the crew reporting moderate turbulence, ice accumulation and precipitation while travelling at about 35,000 feet, requesting to climb, and then reporting a mayday, a descent of 10,000 feet per minute and later an overspeed condition with a descent of 20,000 feet per minute. Those recordings describe what was communicated during the flight; they do not establish why the aircraft lost control. Florida Governor Ron DeSantis, speaking at the scene, described the crash as “cataclysmic,” saying it looked like “a missile going down” and left a large crater beside the road, according to the Associated Press.

On the ground, the outcome could have been worse — and the disruption was still severe. Governor DeSantis said there were no immediate reports of anyone on the ground injured or killed. But the jet came down near the entrance to a mobile home park, and about 600 residents were evacuated and relocated to shelters, Palm Beach County Commissioner Joel Flores said, according to Euronews. Roads were closed in both directions along Military Trail between Cresthaven Boulevard and 10th Avenue North, power was knocked out locally, and the Palm Beach County Fire Rescue described the response as an extended emergency operation. The aircraft was destroyed on impact, with wreckage and fire damage spread across the roadway.

The crash site sits less than 11 kilometres from President Donald Trump's Mar-a-Lago residence, a proximity that drew immediate attention but, on the reported facts, no established connection to the property. Trump had been at Mar-a-Lago on Sunday but departed on Air Force One at 11:55 a.m. from the nearby airport recently renamed for him, about an hour before the Learjet crashed, Euronews reported. No link between the jet and Mar-a-Lago has been suggested by officials, and the aircraft's registered owner was reported to be a Delaware corporate entity, with the jet listed for charter.

The National Transportation Safety Board will lead the investigation, with the FAA sending a team to support it, Secretary Duffy said. NTSB investigators were expected at the site on Sunday and Monday, with a media briefing planned in West Palm Beach on Monday. Investigators will examine flight data, maintenance history, weather conditions and communications to establish what happened. An aviation safety expert quoted by the Associated Press said icing and turbulence could have been factors and may have caused the plane to stall while cruising at 34,000 feet, but stressed that many potential factors remain and that investigators would not rule anything out at this early stage. The NTSB usually releases a preliminary report within about 30 days of a crash; a final finding on the cause often takes a year or more. For the families of the nine people on board, and for the hundreds of residents displaced from their homes, that long process began on Sunday with a fireball on a Florida road — and with no answer yet as to why a jet cruising at altitude fell out of the sky.
