Fuel Tanker Crash Ignites Massive Fire on Cairo's Ring Road, Killing at Least 31

Sezgin Baktiaya · 2026-11-25 · 5 min read

A crash involving a fuel tanker ignited a massive fire on the Ring Road, a key artery encircling the Egyptian capital, on Wednesday evening, killing at least 31 people and injuring 21, Egyptian authorities said. The privately operated tanker, carrying petroleum products, veered off the road and crashed before exploding, according to Egypt's Petroleum Ministry. The fire broke out above Tunnel 10 in the Warraq district of Imbaba, in Giza governorate, near Cairo.

The blaze engulfed more than two dozen cars and motorcycles, and rescue workers pulled people from burning vehicles as firefighters battled the flames spreading among the cars. Hundreds of people gathered near the scene in the densely populated Warraq neighbourhood. Ahmed Sameer, who was stuck in traffic after the road was blocked, said he saw the fuel truck and many cars on fire, and at least eight bodies being removed from burned cars. "It was a huge fire, the cars got stuck together because of the fire," he told the Associated Press. "It was a shocking scene."

Thirty-nine ambulances were deployed to the scene, and the injured were taken to three hospitals, the Health Ministry said. Health Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar raised the state of readiness at hospitals near the site and travelled to the scene and the referral hospitals to monitor the response. The Egyptian Red Crescent said it also deployed emergency response teams to the scene and to the hospitals to support civil defence, ambulance crews and other authorities.

The figures shifted during the day. Early reports put the toll at 30 dead and 15 injured; the Health Ministry later raised it to 31 dead and 21 injured, revising an earlier injured figure of 25 down to 21. Health Minister Abdel Ghaffar said the death toll was preliminary and could rise further. Video published by the Al Arabiya Egypt news outlet showed a large blaze engulfing vehicles on the road, with thick plumes of black smoke rising above the area.

President Abdel-Fattah el-Sissi expressed his condolences to the victims in a statement. The Public Prosecutor's office said DNA investigations were under way to identify the victims. No official cause has yet been given for why the tanker left the road.

Deadly traffic accidents claim thousands of lives every year in Egypt, which has a poor road safety record. Speeding, bad roads and poor enforcement of traffic laws are the main reasons behind most of the crashes, and the Ring Road — which links the governorates of Cairo, Giza and Qalyubia across a metropolitan area of more than 10 million people — is one of the country's busiest highways. In January, 21 people were killed and 16 injured in a massive multicar pileup on a road near the southern province of Beni Suef.
