Nine Dead, 17 Trapped After Flood Swamps an Illegal Gold Mine in Indonesia

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-25 · 5 min read

Rescuers in Indonesia recovered nine bodies from an unauthorised gold mine in Bogor regency, West Java, on Wednesday, and pressed on with the search for 17 other miners believed trapped after heavy rain flooded the shaft late on Monday. The miners were digging for gold when the nearby Ciseureuh River overflowed into a shaft estimated at about 100 metres deep, in a mountainous area south of Jakarta. Eleven miners escaped from one section of the excavation before the floodwaters filled the shaft, according to West Java police.

The rescue began late — about 16 hours after the flooding — because nobody reported the accident. "The problem was that nobody reported it and nobody wanted to report it because the activity was illegal," said Muhammad Adam Hamdani, head of emergency and logistics at the Bogor disaster mitigation agency. Local miners and residents first tried to pump the water out themselves before the situation became unmanageable. Police were told about the trapped miners roughly 16 hours after the flood, and only after a formal report from state-owned miner PT Antam to the Jakarta Search and Rescue Agency shortly after midnight on Wednesday did a full-scale operation begin.

The operation now involves 325 rescuers — emergency workers, police, soldiers, health officials and volunteers — using submersible pumps to drain the shaft and trying to seal off openings where groundwater seeps in. The first victim was recovered shortly after midday on Wednesday, two more about an hour later, and six more by the evening, from two adjacent shafts. All nine were found dead at depths of 30 to 70 metres. "Water inside the hole has not yet receded and is expected to take a long time to drain," said rescue chief Desiana Kartika Bahari, who cited high water levels, narrow access, slippery terrain and limited lighting.

The flooded shaft sits near facilities run by PT Antam's Pongkor gold mining unit, but the company said the accident happened at an unauthorised mine unrelated to its operations. Antam said all of its employees were safe and operations continued normally, adding that it has repeatedly closed off access to illegal mining sites in the Pongkor area and uses patrols, drones and surveillance cameras to deter unauthorised digging. "On humanitarian grounds, Antam is supporting search and evacuation efforts," said company spokesperson Wisnu Danandi Haryanto.

The figures shifted during the day — an earlier Reuters report counted eight dead and 18 trapped, while the Associated Press's later update put it at nine dead and 17 believed trapped — because an unlicensed pit keeps no roster of who was underground. Eleven miners escaped; of the 26 who did not come out, nine are confirmed dead and 17 are still missing. The numbers may change again as the pumps drain the shaft and teams reach deeper levels.

The disaster is the latest fatal accident in Indonesia's informal gold sector, where thousands of people work unlicensed pits with little protection. Landslides, flooding and tunnel collapses are routine hazards, and much of the ore is processed with highly toxic mercury and cyanide, often with little or no protective equipment. For the families waiting at the site, the rescue is now a race against the water — and for the authorities, the accident is a fresh test of how to police illegal digging around the Pongkor mines.
