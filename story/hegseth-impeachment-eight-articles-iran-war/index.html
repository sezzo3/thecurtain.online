Republican Lawmaker Files Eight Articles of Impeachment Against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth Over Iran War

The Curtain Editorial · 2026-11-10 · 3 min read

Representative Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican, introduced eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on Monday, accusing him of waging war against Iran without congressional authorization and abusing the powers of his office. Because Massie offered the resolution as a question of House privilege, Republican leaders have two legislative days to schedule a vote, setting up a possible floor vote as early as Wednesday.

The first three articles accuse Mr Hegseth of conducting the Iran campaign in violation of the War Powers Resolution. They allege that operations began on 28 February without congressional authorisation and continued despite a congressional directive to withdraw and the expiry of statutory deadlines. "By engaging in hostilities in Iran for more than 90 days without congressional authorization, Secretary Hegseth is breaking the law and must be held accountable," Mr Massie said in a statement. The resolution is anchored to H.Con.Res.86, a concurrent resolution directing the removal of US armed forces from hostilities with Iran absent a declaration of war or specific authorisation.

A fourth article alleges failures to protect civilians. It cites a strike on the Shajarah Tayyebeh Elementary School in Minab, Iran, which the resolution says killed or injured more than 200 civilians. The remaining articles concern lethal strikes on suspected drug-trafficking vessels in the Caribbean, alleged retaliation against Senator Mark Kelly after he joined other lawmakers in a video urging service members to refuse unlawful orders, the operation to seize Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, and military action in Yemen. Mr Massie's office said the vessel strikes had killed at least 243 people, updating a figure of at least 221 in its September summary; at least 80 vessels have been destroyed since the operations began last September, with the Pentagon not providing public evidence for its claims against the people killed.

The politics of the vote are unusually open. Republicans control the House 218-214, but the resolution could pass given unified Democratic support, and several House Republicans have told Fox News privately that they believe it could. If the impeachment vote succeeds, Mr Hegseth would be the third cabinet secretary in US history to be impeached. Impeachment by the House would not remove him from office — it amounts to the House formally bringing charges, like an indictment — and conviction in the Senate, a much higher hurdle, is seen as unlikely. Mr Hegseth has denied wrongdoing and President Donald Trump has pledged to stand by him. Mr Hegseth is scheduled to address reporters at the Pentagon on Tuesday at 8 a.m. Eastern to respond to the move.

Mr Massie first brought the articles to the House floor on 15 September, reading a 34-page resolution and offering it as a privileged resolution. The chamber adjourned without voting and did not return until Monday. The renewed push follows the 3 November midterm elections, in which Democrats were projected to win both the House and the Senate, with some Republicans shifting blame for the party's losses toward the Pentagon and the war with Iran. The new Congress takes office on 3 January 2027, and Mr Massie's resolution will expire that day if it does not receive a vote on its merits by then. Mr Massie lost his primary election and will not return to Congress next year, so a different House member would need to reintroduce articles of impeachment at that point.

A separate impeachment resolution against Mr Hegseth also remains pending: Representative Yassamin Ansari introduced H.Res. 1177 in April with six articles. Neither resolution has been adopted by the House. The question now is whether Republican leaders move to table Mr Massie's resolution within the two-day window, or whether the vote — the first on impeaching a cabinet secretary in decades — actually takes place.
