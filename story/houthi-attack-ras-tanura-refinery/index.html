Houthis Attack Saudi Arabia's Largest Refinery, Ras Tanura, as Oil Prices Jump

Sezgin Baktiaya · 2026-11-26 · 5 min read

Yemen's Houthis said on Wednesday they had attacked Saudi Arabia's largest oil refinery, Ras Tanura, and Riyadh's financial district, as satellite images showed thick black smoke over the installation and oil prices jumped. A source with knowledge of the matter confirmed an attack on the refinery, which covers about 600 square kilometres — roughly the size of Chicago.

Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said the strike on the refinery was "accurate and direct" and had caused fires at the site. Satellite imagery from NASA and Planet Labs showed thick black smoke over the complex, and no casualties have been reported. The Saudi government and the state oil firm Aramco did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The Saudi-led coalition fighting the Houthis said earlier on Wednesday that air defences had intercepted and destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura in the Eastern Province. It did not say the refinery had been struck. The coalition said later on Wednesday that a projectile fired by the Houthis fell at Jazan port on the Red Sea, causing minor damage but no civilian injuries, and the Houthis said late on Wednesday they had also attacked the ports of Jeddah and Jazan, though there was no immediate confirmation that Jeddah had been hit.

Ras Tanura processes 550,000 barrels of oil a day — about 16 percent of Saudi Arabia's refining capacity — and sits alongside one of the world's largest crude export terminals. Brent crude settled $3.21, or 3.5 percent, higher at $94.61 a barrel on Wednesday, while US West Texas Intermediate climbed $1.63, or 2 percent, to $83.68, as analysts said the attack raised the geopolitical risk premium and traders covered short positions ahead of the US Thanksgiving holiday.

Saree also said the group attacked Riyadh's financial district in three phases. Saudi civil defence said 11 people were wounded on Tuesday in a strike on a tower in the district — the tallest building in the capital, home to the Public Investment Fund, the kingdom's sovereign wealth fund. It was the second strike on the tower in less than a week: the first, on 19 November, damaged the building but caused no injuries, and civil defence posted an image on Tuesday showing damage about halfway up the tower's facade.

The Houthis, who control much of western Yemen including the capital Sanaa, have been firing at Saudi airports, oil infrastructure and office buildings since Yemen's frozen civil war reignited in July, in a spillover from the US-Iran war. The attacks have previously disrupted some of Aramco's operations, cancelled most international flights to Riyadh and forced Saudi Arabia to postpone major events, and fighting has intensified since the group captured strategic areas along Yemen's Red Sea coast in September, advancing towards the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a key shipping route for Gulf oil.
