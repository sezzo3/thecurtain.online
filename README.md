# The Curtain — thecurtain.online

**News with context, clarity and care.**

[thecurtain.online](https://thecurtain.online) is an independent digital news publication covering world affairs, politics, business, technology and society. It is run by one person: no newsroom, no sponsors, no salary behind it. Every story is researched, written and published independently.

## Why I built this

I got tired of news that tells you what happened in the first paragraph and then fills the rest with noise — hot takes, recycled quotes, and speculation dressed up as fact.

I wanted a place that does the opposite:

- **Report what is known** — and say plainly what isn't known yet.
- **Explain why it matters** — the context, the chronology, the stakes.
- **Show the workings** — every important claim linked to its source, so you can check it yourself.

The Curtain is that place. Short, sourced, and honest about uncertainty.

## What you'll find here

- **Daily news** — the top stories of the day, published twice daily, each one a complete, self-contained read.
- **History features** — longer, story-driven pieces on the events and people that shaped the world we live in.
- **Four languages** — every new story is published in English, Turkish, German and Spanish.

## How I research a story

No single source tells the whole story. So I don't rely on one. My process for every story looks like this:

### 1. Start with reputable reporting

I begin with established, reputable outlets and primary sources — official statements, documents, and on-the-ground reporting. For breaking news, I don't publish on a single report: a story needs corroboration from **two or more reputable outlets** before it goes on the site.

### 2. Check the prediction markets — Polymarket and Kalshi

Then I look at what real money is saying. On **Polymarket** and **Kalshi**, people stake actual money on outcomes — an election result, a ceasefire date, a central bank decision — so the prices are a live read on what a large, motivated crowd expects to happen.

I use those prices as **a signal, not a verdict**. A 70% price on a ceasefire doesn't mean a ceasefire will happen; it means the crowd, with money on the line, thinks it's more likely than not. That is useful context for a reader — it shows where expectations sit, how they move as events unfold, and where the surprises are. It never replaces reporting, and it never appears in a story as a fact on its own.

My background is in risk, fraud and cybersecurity, so I think in probabilities by habit. Prediction markets fit that way of thinking: they force the question *"how likely is this, really?"* instead of *"who shouts loudest about it?"*

### 3. Listen to Reddit and Quora

After the reporting and the markets, I read what ordinary people are saying — mainly **Reddit** threads and **Quora** discussions about the story.

That serves two purposes:

- **The general mood.** What are people actually worried about, confused by, or arguing over? The comments often show which part of a story matters to real readers, and which questions the reporting hasn't answered yet.
- **The small, local details.** Someone in the city where it happened, someone who works in the industry, someone who was there. A local eyewitness account, a detail about a neighbourhood, a first-hand explanation of how something works on the ground — the kind of thing that never makes it into a wire report.

I treat everything from these platforms as a **lead, not a fact**. A comment is never quoted as evidence on its own; if a detail from Reddit or Quora is worth including, it gets checked against reporting first, and it is presented for what it is — what people are saying, not what is confirmed.

### 4. Verify, attribute, and say what's uncertain

Before a story is published, every important claim is traced back to the people, institutions or records behind it. Confirmed facts, allegations, estimates and analysis are kept separate. If independent verification is limited, the story says so. Sources are linked on the names themselves, so you can follow any claim back to where it came from.

### 5. Write it clearly

The finished story is written in plain, professional language — the facts first, the context second, the uncertainty stated, not buried. No clickbait, no filler.

## Corrections

If a material factual error is confirmed, I correct it and explain significant changes on the article itself. If you spot an error or have a documented correction, send it with the article link and I'll review it.
